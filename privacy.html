Privacy

Read the policy

Your data, plainly explained.

This page explains in plain words what data stays on your computer, what the Oak server sees, what your RPC and ecosystem project services see, how long data is kept, and how to ask questions. The compiled add-on and core packages are free to use under the Oak package license; their implementation source and the hosted service are private. Oak is free. Every action needs your yes in chat before local signing.

1. Website Assets and Requests

Read policy details

Oak's public website uses self-hosted fonts and images. It has no analytics, advertising trackers, tracking cookies, forms or iframes.

The Agents page (agents.html) uses a small JavaScript script to request Agent 1's public tracker from https://api.oakagentkit.org/v1/agents/1/tracker. The request omits credentials and sends no wallet information. Oak's API receives the visitor's IP address and normal request metadata.

The other pages do not request external data.

2. What the Add-On Keeps Locally on Your Computer

HEY Research Lab on your computer. Oak keeps a session cache of HEY Research Lab answers in memory; stopping the session clears that cache. Any answer included in an action summary follows the prepared-action and chat data flows described above. The builder feed keeps your follow list and last-checked times in ~/.oak/hey-follow.json on your computer (see the table above). Oak never uploads that file or the checked times. When you ask for your feed, only the token addresses and project slugs on the list are used to ask HEY Research Lab for updates (see "What each other HEY Research Lab tool sends" below). Following or unfollowing a token contacts no one.

Agent Wormhole on your computer. Oak integrates Agent Wormhole checks. Agent Wormhole's own team runs its product and contracts; Oak didn't build and doesn't run their checks. The automatic offline text gate checks selected third-party strings in requested tool results in the local process, before agent use and approvals. It scans link text without visiting destinations. The offline gate itself uploads nothing. Action summaries go to your MCP client and AI provider so your agent can show them in chat. Summaries can contain public addresses, token amounts, fees, approvals, warnings and selected partner context. Your private key and recovery words are not included. Diagnostic evidence is no longer echoed into the agent's tool result text; instead, tool results carry a compact one-line verdict and evidenceBlockId (plus up to 5 sanitized flagged field paths when not clear). The bridge keeps the full per-field diagnostic evidence objects (field paths and 64-hex input hashes rather than raw excerpts; hashes can still identify known content) in memory for the running session only, with at most 1,000 blocks and oldest-entry eviction; stopping the bridge clears them. The local read-only tool oak_wormhole_evidence (and wormholeEvidence(id) in code) retrieves that same evidence object by evidenceBlockId during the session. These objects are never written to disk or added to the persistent approval-receipt file. Callers and the agent client may retain returned results under their own policies. Allow is not proof of safety.

Agent Wormhole hosted checks. These are on by default during eligible requests: the selected third-party text goes to Oak's server and Agent Wormhole. Set OAK_WORMHOLE_PAYER_MODE=off to opt out; this keeps offline scans and free metadata snapshots available. Free swap token metadata snapshots (wormhole_token_check and swap quote checks) send chain ID 4663 and the ERC-20 token address to Oak's server and Agent Wormhole (dashboard.agentwormhole.com), while hosted text checks run only while an action prepares its approval (never on ordinary reads).

Portfolio, accounting exports, diagnostics, and plan execution on your computer. oak_portfolio and oak_receipts {"operation":"export","format":"csv","view":"accounting"} read your local 0600 receipt and journal files along with chain and hosted position reads, and send that financial view to your MCP client and AI model provider (which leaves your computer under their retention policies). For a local 18-column accounting CSV that never enters the chat, run oak receipts export accounting-csv in your own terminal. oak_claimable reads across reward programs (merkl, chop, pons, clanker, longbow, orbio, tickerspring, hoodlock, and uniswap) using the normal wallet-bound read path without preparing or sending transactions. oak_whois and oak_doctor read through your configured RPC (the RPC sees the queried 0x address and trace probe; oak_doctor also checks ETH and USDG prices, official stock USD estimates, and hosted account status, with no new third party). oak_dry_run builds, checks and simulates 1 to 8 transaction actions without signing or writing a receipt. oak_plan_run prepares 1 to 8 eligible transaction actions on Robinhood Chain (4663) for one chat confirmation listing every step and limit; after your yes, oak_execute re-checks the steps, signs locally and records decision receipts in ~/.oak/approval-receipts.jsonl. The Merkl card reader uses Oak's hosted merkl_campaigns read, sending the pool's campaign query and not your wallet address.

What never leaves your computer:

  • Recovery words, if you create or recover a wallet with owner-run oak init, stay in your own terminal. Automatic wallets have no recovery phrase. Terminal logging or screen recording can retain displayed words.
  • Your private key and passphrase (wiped from memory buffers with .fill(0) after unlocking or signing). Oak never asks for the private key of an external NFT owner. That owner signs an OakIdentityLink challenge in your terminal during oak setup link <agentId> or oak identity unlink --owner <linkId>, and only the signature is sent to the Oak server (section 3). Never paste a key or recovery words at that prompt.
  • Secret rejection checks argument field names, not all free-text values. Never paste credentials into work text, conversations or receipts: such text may be forwarded. Both the local bridge (packages/addon/src/bridge.ts) and the Oak server reject any tool argument containing private_key, secret_key, wallet_key, mnemonic, seed, seed_phrase, or recovery_phrase.

3. What the Oak Server Sees

Read policy details

When the add-on talks to your configured Oak service URL, the server receives only:

  1. Authenticated tool calls (POST /v1/tools/:name):
    • Your wallet address and session signature (X-Oak-Session): A base64url-encoded JSON payload {"expires":<unix-seconds>,"wallet":"<EIP-55-address>","sessionKey"?:"<EIP-55-address>","identity"?:{"chainId":<id>,"registry":"<address>","agentId":"<id>","linkId"?:"0x..."},"nonce"?:"0x<32-byte-hex>"} (lifetime up to 900 seconds / 15 minutes) signed with EIP-191 by your wallet (packages/addon/src/session.ts).
    • Single-use request signature (X-Oak-Request): Sent on non-read tool calls. A base64url-encoded JSON payload {"session":"<sha256-of-X-Oak-Session>","method":"POST","path":"<request-path>","bodyHash":"<sha256-of-raw-body>","nonce":"0x<32-byte-hex>","expires":<unix-seconds>} (lifetime up to 60 seconds, capped by session expiry) signed with EIP-191 by the ephemeral in-memory sessionKey bound inside X-Oak-Session (packages/addon/src/session.ts). Used to block replayed requests and enforce per-link and per-session request limits.
    • The tool name and JSON arguments: For example, the token address and base-unit amount you asked to quote, preview, or build (<= 1 MiB), or a locally signed EIP-712 RFQ approval during a meridian_buy / meridian_sell continuation.
  2. ERC-8004 identity setup, status, and unlinking (POST /v1/identity/challenge, /v1/identity/link, /v1/identity/status, /v1/identity/unlink):
    • Session and request headers: X-Oak-Session on all four endpoints, plus X-Oak-Request (up to 60 seconds, single use) on challenge, link, and unlink (packages/addon/src/identity.ts).
    • Identity tuple and owner challenge proof: The ERC-8004 identity fields (chainId, registry, agentId, and optional linkId), and on link or owner-initiated unlink, the single-use EIP-712 OakIdentityLink challenge (300-second window) and the NFT owner's signature (signature) proving ownership or revocation (packages/core/src/identity-link.ts).
    • Client IP address for challenge rate limiting (POST /v1/identity/challenge): When /v1/identity/challenge is called without an already-verified active session, the server uses the client IP address (from the direct connection or the local reverse-proxy header X-Oak-Client-IP, with IPv6 addresses grouped by /64 prefix) solely to enforce an in-memory rate limit of 10 challenges per 60 seconds per IP bucket (across up to 1,024 buckets in memory).
  3. Open price-note requests (POST /v1/price-note):
    • Burner target: {"for":"burner"}, with X-Oak-Session authenticating the caller's wallet.
    • Peer IP address for rate limiting: The server uses the direct connection IP address, accepting X-Oak-Client-IP only from the trusted local reverse proxy (which supplies the real client address), and ignoring untrusted forwarded-IP headers, solely to enforce the 60-second in-memory rate limit (10 requests/minute per IP, 120 requests/minute global).
  4. Health checks (GET /v1/health): Responds with {"ok":true,"data":{"status":"ok"}} when the service is healthy.
  5. Free access: Oak is free. Read tools need no agent identity. Actions require an ERC-8004 identity and your yes in chat, except the free Gloam claim, which needs your yes but no identity.
  6. Deployment settings (GET /v1/config): When the add-on starts, and again on its hourly check, it asks the Oak server for the public deployment addresses so it can compare them with the addresses pinned in the add-on. This request carries no wallet address, session header or tool data.
  7. Agent Wormhole hosted checks (POST /v1/wormhole/check): On by default for every user during an eligible current request in server-key mode. Set OAK_WORMHOLE_PAYER_MODE=off to opt out. Oak receives bounded selected third-party text and a price cap, plus the wallet session and a single-use request signature bound to the path and body. The server verifies the wallet-signed session and request proof, then applies hosted-service budgets and rate limits. Only the text is forwarded as {content: text} in POST /api/v1/check to Agent Wormhole, with Oak's prepaid awk_ API key in the Authorization header: no wallet identity, session headers, signatures, private keys, user prompts or complete tool envelopes. A missing key, exhausted credit, HTTP 402 or other failure gives back unknown; no per-check payment is signed or retried. The price is $0.005000 USDC per metered text check (observed on the account ledger 2026-11-05); the $0.003000 headline is the verify endpoint. The default admission cap remains 5000 microusd; a lower effective cap refuses before provider contact. chargedMicrousd reports the stated price for a usable response, not a per-request billing receipt.

The Oak server never sees your private key, never signs or sends a blockchain transaction, and has no general user account database, email list, or telemetry SDK. Identity links, request replay records and operational logs have the retention described in section 5.

Gloam relay and deposit attribution

Gloam is an official Oak partner. Gloam's own team runs its product and contracts; Oak didn't build them and doesn't run them.

  • Oak relay forward: /v1/gloam/relay receives only public proof fields, roots, nullifiers and commitments; cash-outs also name the wallet recipient and amount. The add-on sends no Oak session or request signature. The server sees the computer's IP while handling the request, forwards the public payload to https://gloam.trade/api/v1/relay using a server-held service key, and logs no body, recipient, nullifier, commitment or IP. It never receives note secrets or receive keys.
  • Deposit attribution: After a shield is confirmed, /v1/gloam/deposits forwards its public transaction hash and chain ID to Gloam. That hash can be linked to the wallet on chain. Failed attribution is retried only on a later Gloam request within 24 hours, never in the background. No session is sent.
  • Circuit download: The first requested Gloam action downloads pinned proving files from www.gloam.trade. Gloam sees the computer's internet address on download; later calls use the hash-checked cache.
  • Public relay fallback: Before you confirm a Gloam relay action, the action summary explains: If Oak's server can't be reached, Oak sends this through Gloam's public relay instead, and Gloam then sees your computer's internet address. Only an unreachable Oak forward or a 5xx response permits the SDK's public relay path. Gloam then sees the computer's internet address. A 4xx refuses; Oak never broadcasts a private relay action from the wallet. The result identifies the route used. Gloam's own retention policy applies to requests it receives; Oak does not establish its deletion behavior.
  • Retention: The forward keeps no payload or persistent request record. Per-IP attempt timestamps enforce a rolling 60-second window in memory; expired timestamps are removed on a later request, and process exit discards them. There is a shared 10/minute IP limit and 240/minute global ceiling. Circuit and tree caches contain no note secrets. Claim scans keep their cursor encrypted in the local store.

4. What Chain RPCs and Ecosystem Project Services See

Read policy details

Blockchain RPCs (OAK_RPC_URL and OAK_RPC_URL_<chainId>)

  • Your local add-on uses Oak's hosted /v1/rpc by default on mainnet for Robinhood Chain (4663) and Base (8453) reads, simulation and tracing. Explicit per-chain RPC settings take priority; OAK_RPC_URL selects a direct mainnet RPC. Signed transaction broadcasts and approval-history scans use a direct RPC. Ordinary reads may fall back to the public chain RPC under the conditions and warnings described in “Hosted RPC visibility and trust” below.
  • The Oak server connects to its configured RPC providers to read chain state, verify ERC-8004 ownership and identity links, read OakOracle, and simulate requests. Those providers see the method and parameters Oak forwards.
  • Any RPC provider you configure can see the IP address making those RPC requests, the wallet address queried, and any signed transaction broadcast through it. Once a transaction is broadcast to Robinhood Chain (4663) or a Relay origin chain, its sender, calldata, and events are public and permanent on the blockchain.

5. Retention and Deletion

Read policy details
  • On your computer (~/.oak/):
    • Most local files have no automatic age limit. Wallets, replaced-wallet backups, old file keys, config, recipes, received-token hints, cleared records, historical daily-spending reservations, receipt logs, Gloam notes, tree/circuit caches and opaque attribution markers remain until managed by the owner. Pending signed transactions clear only after finality or proven recovery; approval cleanup records are forgotten on revoke. Signature history is pruned only on later signing reconciliation. Never delete recovery state for unresolved transactions.
    • The saved identity record in ~/.oak/config.json (chainId, registry, agentId, and optional linkId) stays in your config while your ERC-8004 identity link is valid and is cleared automatically by the add-on when you run oak identity unlink or when the server reports that the identity was unlinked, revoked, transferred, or burned (it is kept across temporary RPC or server outages so a retry does not drop your link).
    • Gloam note secrets and the receive key are spend authority. Losing both the store and its backup loses the money. A backup covers only the notes present when made; never send it through an agent.
    • Raeto keys in ~/.oak/raeto.json persist until removed with oak raeto forget in your terminal or overwritten by a new sign-in on top-up. The Raeto ledger (~/.oak/raeto-ledger.jsonl), payments journal (~/.oak/raeto-payments.json), and limits file (~/.oak/raeto-settings.json) remain on disk as local records until deleted by the owner.
    • Remember to back up your recovery words or run oak export before deleting ~/.oak/wallet.enc, because Oak cannot recover a lost key.
  • On the Oak server:
    • ERC-8004 identity store (/var/lib/oak/identity.json, file mode 0600):
      • Active and recently revoked links (links): Stores each verified link record (id, the OakIdentityLink payload with chainId, registry, agentId, agentWallet, audience, challenge, issuedAt, and expiresAt, plus owner, proofHash, blockNumber, blockHash, consumedAt, optional checkedThrough, and optional revokedAt; raw owner signatures are never stored). Active links are kept while the link remains active so the server can authorize the linked agent wallet without re-prompting the NFT owner; revoked or superseded links (revokedAt) are kept for 24 hours (86,400 seconds) so a revoked link cannot be replayed or revived, and are then pruned on the next store cleanup.
      • Request replay records (requests): Stores consumed single-use X-Oak-Request records (key digest of session and request nonce, expires, wallet, linkId, session SHA-256 hash of X-Oak-Session, and usedAt timestamp). Kept until the parent session expires (up to 900 seconds / 15 minutes) to block replayed X-Oak-Request headers and enforce per-link and per-session request limits (4 concurrent sessions per link, 180 requests per session, 60 requests per 60 seconds), and then pruned.
      • Hosted-check replay records (recoveryRequests): When Agent Wormhole hosted checks run with the durable request store, this holds the session/nonce digest (key), expiry, public wallet, session hash and use time. These records survive restart and remain until parent session expiry (at most 15 minutes), pruned on store cleanup. The store's per-wallet and per-session quotas also apply. No submitted text, raw session header or API key is retained here.
      • Consumed challenge proofs (proofs): Stores SHA-256 digests of consumed OakIdentityLink challenges (chainId, registry, agentId, digest, expiresAt, purpose, and optional linkId). Kept until the challenge window expires ( 300 seconds / 5 minutes, capped at 32 link proofs per identity) so a signed owner challenge cannot be replayed, and then pruned.
    • Session headers (X-Oak-Session): Verified in memory; the raw header is never written to disk, and only its SHA-256 hash inside identity.json request records is kept for up to 900 seconds (15 minutes) until session expiry.
    • In-memory rate-limit counters: Price-note per-IP and global timestamps (10/minute per IP, 120/minute global) and /v1/identity/challenge per-IP buckets (10 per 60 seconds per IP or IPv6 /64 prefix across up to 1,024 buckets) are held in process memory only and expire after 60 seconds.
    • Agent Wormhole hosted checks: The adapter writes no raw-text or credential check log. Its process-local rate windows hold the authenticated session wallet, window start and attempt count, with a default of 16 attempts per 60 seconds and at most 10,000 buckets. They expire on a later request or process exit. Paid checks require the durable replay store described above (ServerOptions.wormholeRequestStore, defaulting to identity.store). Without that store, the route verifies requests with a bounded in-memory nonce fence but gives back unknown and sends no paid request. The rate windows remain local to one server process; multi-worker deployments need a shared limiter and an atomic shared replay store. Normal request authentication and the service logs above still apply. This does not establish Agent Wormhole's storage, deletion or retention policy for text, metadata or request records.
    • Meridian RFQ continuation state: Held in process memory only while the short multi-turn auction handoff is active; discarded immediately when the exchange completes, expires, or disconnects.
    • Issued price-note nonces (/var/lib/oak/nonces.jsonl, file mode 0600): Random 32-byte hex nonces (0x...) are appended to disk and kept indefinitely for the lifetime of the deployment so the price-note issuer never reissues a nonce across restarts.
    • Aggregate health metrics (/var/lib/oak/metrics.json, file mode 0600): Rolling 5-minute aggregate counters (requests, serverErrors, clientErrors, process rss memory, and timestamp at) with no IPs, wallets, headers, or request bodies; overwritten every 60 seconds for local service health checks.
    • State backups: Each hourly run creates a temporary plaintext 0600 bundle in /var/lib/oak/backups/.pending.*, verifies it, and uploads encrypted off-host snapshots (restic) of /var/lib/oak/identity.json and /var/lib/oak/nonces.jsonl so active identity links and issued price-note nonces survive a host failure without resetting replay fences. The run removes only its own plaintext staging bundle on normal success, failure or a handled signal. A forced kill or power loss can leave a bundle; older bundles from previous versions also remain for operator-approved cleanup. Encrypted remote snapshots follow operator-managed retention; expiry in the live identity store does not erase historical backups.
    • Retired grant records: Historical grant records may remain in operator-managed backups. Current Oak does not issue or redeem access grants. Backup expiry follows operator retention.
    • System logs (systemd-journald, /etc/systemd/journald.conf.d/oak.conf): Operational service and reverse-proxy logs (capped at 100 MB on disk and 32 MB in runtime memory, with no HTTP request header or body access logs) are kept for at most 7 days (MaxRetentionSec=7day) for service troubleshooting and then rotated out automatically. Launch and private-test events include the event name and tool only, with no wallet address, request body or headers. These are repository template settings; live host configuration and partner retention were not inspected.
  • On the blockchain: Transactions, token balances, .hood names, and ERC-8004 agent registrations on Robinhood Chain (4663) — including the mandatory public oak.registrar = oak metadata marker written in the registration transaction when you register a new identity through Oak (oak setup register, oak register, oak_setup, or erc8004_register) — are permanent public ledger records and remain in public chain history even if metadata is changed later; they cannot be deleted by Oak.
    • Raeto states that it does not retain prompts or model completions; requests forwarded to model labs follow each provider's policy. Raeto account balances, key records and receipts are held by Raeto under its own policies; only paired ERC-8004 weekly statements and payment transactions published on Robinhood Chain are permanent public on-chain records.
    • Factor agent profiles, pay links (capped at 30 days), job applications, and public work texts (show: true) published to Factor's engine become public and cannot be edited. On-chain FactorDesk events, jobs, deliveries, and draw records on Robinhood Chain (4663) are permanent public blockchain records.

Automatic wallets have no mnemonic and require no passphrase. File fallback keys persist beside the encrypted wallet until managed by the owner; readers of both files can unlock the wallet. Export/recovery are optional owner-terminal commands, and existing wallets are never silently migrated.

Hosted RPC visibility and trust

Read policy details

Hosted RPC lets Oak see your wallet and transactions being checked. Without your own RPC, guards rely on Oak's RPC answers. Keys and signing stay local; signed transactions go directly to a chain RPC.

A fresh mainnet add-on uses Oak's session-budgeted /v1/rpc for reads, simulation and tracing on Robinhood Chain (4663) and Base (8453). An explicit OAK_RPC_URL_4663 or OAK_RPC_URL selects your direct mainnet RPC; OAK_RPC_URL_8453 selects your direct Base RPC. Per-chain settings take priority over the generic mainnet setting. These settings do not bypass Oak-hosted tools or keyed Relay/KyberSwap services. Core/server clients retain direct configuration and never recursively call the hosted default.

The add-on signs a short-lived wallet session and a single-use proof of each exact RPC envelope locally. These bearer/proof headers remain in memory and go only to the selected Oak server over HTTPS (local development may use loopback HTTP). Oak forwards JSON-RPC method/params to its configured provider without forwarding Oak auth headers or caller IP. The provider key remains server-side. Oak does not persist RPC bodies in its application store; replay records retain wallet/session digests up to session expiry (15 minutes). The shared hosted budget persists HMAC wallet/IP cost buckets across restart, with previous-day entries replaced on the next admitted request after UTC midnight. Backup retention and reverse-proxy or upstream-provider logging are controlled by their operators.

When hosted RPC is disabled, rate-limited, out of budget or unreachable, ordinary reads may use the public chain RPC. That provider then sees your IP and query. These reads include contract calls, code, storage, logs, nonces and balances used by later signatures. The prepared approval summary includes a public-RPC warning, retained even if a later hosted simulation succeeds. The warning is shown again before an action signature; fallback first encountered after approval stops signing and requires a fresh summary and yes. The warning conservatively remains for this running process. Public fallback is not used for simulation, gas estimation or tracing, and does not turn a failed check into a successful one. Invalid/mismatched replies and wrong chains are refused. Before any signed broadcast through the default add-on transport, the direct endpoint must answer for the selected chain. Signing, transaction journaling and broadcasting stay on your computer; the signed transaction goes directly to your own RPC or the chain's public RPC, without Oak session headers. The pre-wallet approval-history origin sample and census also use direct RPCs.

For keyed Relay/KyberSwap reads Oak sees the wallet, tokens, amounts, filters and request/event queries, and the selected service receives its server-held key. KyberSwap route handles/summary state remain in bounded server memory until expiry, eviction or process exit; bodies and credentials are not stored by the shared budget. Partner retention remains unmeasured. A mainnet Oak install provides hosted tracing by default on Robinhood Chain (4663) and Base (8453); an own RPC overrides that chain's hosted connection. Other chains require OAK_RPC_URL_<chainId> pointing to a tracing RPC.
